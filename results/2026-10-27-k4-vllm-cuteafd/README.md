## In short

- **K4 against K3.25 is a quantization comparison.** On the same panels and the same top-20 definition, K4's 4-bit
  experts sit roughly half as far from the BF16 teacher as K3.25's 3.25-bit experts, on both engines (P-A KL20: vLLM
  0.0365 → 0.0192, 0.53 of K3.25; CuteAFD 0.0380 →
  0.0219, 0.58). No interval is computed for the change between checkpoints; at
  -0.0173 (vLLM) and -0.0161 (CuteAFD) it is several times either engine
  difference.
- **Engines on K4: vLLM stays closer to BF16; CuteAFD is faster.** CuteAFD is further from the reference on both
  panels (P-A +0.0027, P-B generated rows +0.0053), most in long code reading
  (8-16K +0.0090); on human-written text at 32K-128K no NLL difference is resolved. CuteAFD
  decodes 1.05-1.38x as fast at one stream and 1.11-1.59x at
  four (faster in 20 of 22 cells, level in 2, slower in 0). Its first token comes sooner at one stream from 8K up and at 8K × 4; vLLM's comes sooner at 1K and at
  32K × 4.
- **The engine difference reads larger on K4 than on K3.25** (P-B 8-16K, top-20: +0.0090
  [+0.0062, +0.0122] against +0.0052 [+0.0031, +0.0075]; the intervals overlap, and the
  change between checkpoints was not tested). On K4 CuteAFD converts K4's BF16 projections, head and KDA projections to
  FP8 at load; on K3.25 the projections came from the official FP8 checkpoint.
- **W8A16 prefill moves less of it on K4** (follow-up F1-K4, outside the frozen protocol): P-B 8-16K
  -0.0019 [-0.0039, -0.0002], about 21% of the K4 difference (on K3.25 the same
  switch moved about 59% of its difference). On P-A, CuteAFD with W8A16 is not resolved from vLLM
  (+0.0009 [-0.0014, +0.0028]). Time to first token +3-4%.
- **Long output (Block L): see the Block L table below (descriptive).**
