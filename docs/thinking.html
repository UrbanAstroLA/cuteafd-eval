Investigation: does the 6-bit core make K4-Core-K6 think longer?

On one engine and these questions, storing the core of K4-Core-K6 in 6 bits rather than BF16 shows no measurable effect on when the model stops thinking. On fixed reasoning traces, the probability of closing the reasoning moves less with the BF16 core (+0.005) than between two launches of the same 6-bit model (+0.006). Finished replies to one long-thinking question are the same length within about 1.3x. One of 24 free-generation replies looped to the 327,680-token cap, on the 6-bit core. Whether the core changes how often replies loop is open. The routed experts, which hold most of the parameters, were not part of this test.

What was tested

  • Arms. One checkpoint, wrldsuksgo2mars/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-K4-Core-K6 (K4 routed experts, 6-bit EXL3 core and head). The 6-bit arm rebuilds its 316 core tensors to BF16 at load (exact arithmetic, one rounding). Each swap arm loads the named group's original tensors from zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash-BF16 @ f9fed3bb, the checkpoint's own source, instead; every launch logged the expected number of swapped tensors. Same engine, image, loader and kernels on every arm.
  • Engine. tpurtell's vLLM fork v0.9.2 release image with a local loader modification (not a tpurtell release; the release does not load this checkpoint): repro/vllm-k6-loader.
  • G2, fixed traces. 77 finished and 16 unfinished reasoning traces, each teacher-forced through every arm: 52 from earlier hard-question probes (questions 13, 79, 88, 121, 127; K3.25 and K4 on earlier fork versions, so off-policy here) and 41 generated by the 6-bit arm itself on the seven length-variable questions. Top-64 log-probabilities at sampled rows and at the last 512 rows before each close, plus the exact log-probability of </think> at every stored row. Plus the P-A teacher panel (25 × 2,048 tokens).
  • Protocols. G2's reading rule was frozen before the first capture; a second launch of the 6-bit arm was added (Amendment 1) before any capture was read, because the fork's long-prompt prefill varies between launches. G4 was frozen after G2 was read and before its first sample. The loop checks were chosen after G4 was read and are exploratory.

Open questions

  • Does the 6-bit core change how often replies loop? Measured: 1 loop in 18 samples of question 12 on the 6-bit core (6 earlier, 12 in G4), 0 in 12 on the BF16 core (Fisher p = 1.0); on the seven questions, K4-Core-K6 reached the cap about as often as K4 and K3.25 in earlier records (table above). The per-token stop measures do not see a difference.
  • Do the routed experts' quantization shape thinking length? Unmeasured here: only the core was swapped.
  • Do these results hold on CuteAFD? Unmeasured: its native 6-bit arithmetic differs from the loader's rounding.

Caveats

  • One engine. The loader rounds the 6-bit core once from exact arithmetic; CuteAFD runs it natively and the producer's own rebuild rounds at FP16 between stages (15.8% of elements differ, 2.4% by more than one BF16 unit). Transfer to CuteAFD is not measured.
  • KL here is a top-64 measure with the arm's 64th value as the floor for tokens outside its top 64 (a lower bound), not full vocabulary. GLM tokenizer throughout; not comparable with other model families.
  • G4 is one question; the knife-edge questions where replies tip between short and capped were dropped from G4 because their spread would need hundreds of samples per arm.
