CuteAFD and tpurtell's vLLM fork on two RTX PRO 6000s

On two RTX PRO 6000s, CuteAFD is the faster engine and tpurtell's vLLM fork is the slightly more precise one; one CuteAFD setting (W8A16 prefill) halves the precision difference for about 4% slower first token.

Both engines are tpurtell's. Same model (GLM-5.3-Flash with his 3.25-bit EXL3 experts), same machine, measured under a frozen protocol with an independent audit. Every number comes from the receipts in the repository.

Precision
0.045 vs 0.048

Both engines stay close to the original BF16 model; the 3.25-bit quantization matters more than the engine. The vLLM fork is slightly closer, mainly when reading long code (8-16K tokens).

KL from BF16, vLLM fork vs CuteAFD, tpurtell's fidelity set. His official FP8 model scores 0.0216 on the same set. At 8-16K: 0.0504 vs 0.0557.
Speed
+2% to +41%

CuteAFD generates faster for a single user and finishes long-prompt work 6-17% faster. The vLLM fork answers short prompts sooner (0.25 vs 0.41 s to first token at 1K).

Single-stream decode, CuteAFD vs vLLM fork, 12 cells from 1K to 128K input; single cells vary by up to ±13% between runs.
Long outputs
7/8 and 7/8

Both ran 100 long-output requests with 0 errors. At the 128K cap, code passed every hidden test in 7 of 8 replies on both engines, and math was 8/8 on both.

Two seeds per prompt, four prompts per content type: small samples.
Tunable trade-off
+0.0033 → +0.0017

CuteAFD's W8A16 prefill halves its overall precision difference for about 4% longer time to first token and no decode cost; with all three FP8 switches off no difference is resolved, at about 4% slower median decode.

KL difference from the vLLM fork, all generated rows. At 8-16K: +0.0054 → +0.0022. Prefill-shaped scoring only.
Practical differences
0.017 s

CuteAFD restores a repeated 8K prompt from its prefix cache in 0.017 s; the vLLM fork reuses only whole 4,352-token blocks and none below about 13K tokens. Only CuteAFD honours "thinking off". Switching models: CuteAFD about 1.5 min, the vLLM fork about 6.4 min with its JIT caches persisted (11.6 min without).

The results in pictures

Which to use when

  • CuteAFD for single-user and agent-style work with long or repeated contexts. Single-stream decode is 2-41% faster, long prompts prefill at about 4,400-4,500 against 3,700-3,900 tokens per second, and a repeated prompt comes back from its cache in 0.017 s. Limit: with many long prompts at once, its first tokens come later (32K input, 16 streams: 106 vs 72 s), because it schedules prefills ahead of decode.
  • The vLLM fork where long-code precision matters most, or for many short concurrent prompts. It is closer to BF16 at 8-16K (+0.0054 KL in its favour), answers 1K prompts sooner (0.25 vs 0.41 s), and at 1K input with 8 streams completes 7-15% more tokens per second.
  • CuteAFD with W8A16 prefill as a middle option (GLM5_FLASH_FP8_PREFILL=off): about half of the precision difference goes (+0.0033 → +0.0017 overall), for about 4% longer time to first token and no measured decode cost.

One machine: two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q GPUs at their 300 W power cap, one model (GLM-5.3-Flash, EXL3 K3.25 experts), thinking on. Other GPUs, quantizations or settings can change the picture.

How sure are we

Solid

  • The precision difference is paired against three vLLM runs from two launches, and survives a leave-one-window-out check and a multiple-comparison correction (independent audit).
  • CuteAFD is bit-deterministic: every repeat was byte-identical.
  • Speed did not drift: repeats hours later were within ±2% at the median on both engines.
  • Both engines ran every request with 0 errors.

Thin

  • Long output: two seeds per prompt, four prompts per content type.
  • Long-document NLL (32K-128K): six documents.
  • The BF16 reference stops at 14.4K tokens; beyond it the engines are compared only with each other.
  • Precision was scored in prefill shape; the decode path that generates tokens was not scored.
  • Single speed cells vary by up to ±13% between runs.

Terms

KL (Kullback-Leibler divergence)
How far an engine's next-token probabilities are from the original BF16 model. 0 means identical; lower is better. Measured in nats. tpurtell's fidelity set treats a paired change below 0.005 as within its bar.
Top-1
How often the engine's most likely next token is the reference's most likely one.
NLL
Negative log-likelihood of the actual text: lower means the engine found the text less surprising.
TTFT
Time to first token: from sending a request to the first generated token; mostly prompt processing.
Prefill
Processing the prompt before generation starts. Decode: generating tokens one step at a time.
Stream
One request in flight. Aggregate: all completion tokens of a batch divided by its wall time.
W8A8 / W8A16
FP8 weights with FP8 or 16-bit activations in CuteAFD's prefill matrix multiplies.
