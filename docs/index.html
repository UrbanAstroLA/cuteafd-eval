Engines and quantizations of GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000s

This site measures two inference engines by the same author, CuteAFD and tpurtell's vLLM fork, and several quantized checkpoints of GLM-5.3-Flash, on one machine: two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q GPUs at 300 W. The aim is evidence for choosing a deployment: what each engine and each precision cut costs or buys in distance from the original BF16 model, task accuracy at long context, thinking length, speed and memory. Every answer below is a judgement across studies with a stated strength; each links to the findings it rests on and states what it does not show. Nothing here is a recommendation.

Answers so far

Strength: established (pre-set rules agree on two or more independent item sets, launches or engines), supported (a pre-set rule on one item set, not contradicted), indicated (found after looking, or exploratory), open (mixed or missing). A judgement is never stronger than its weakest essential finding. Definitions are on the terms page.

Where does most of a configuration's distance from the BF16 model come from: the routed experts, the core, or the engine?Mostly from the routed experts: 3.25-bit to 4-bit experts moved teacher-panel KL by −0.0195; the engine moved it by +0.0035 at most and the core by less.establishedprecision
On the same weights, is CuteAFD farther from the BF16 model than the vLLM fork?Slightly: +0.0017 (K3.25) and +0.0035 (K4) on the teacher panel, +0.0054 and +0.0090 on long code; not detected below 8K or in NLL at 32K-128K.establishedprecision
How much of CuteAFD's long-code gap do its FP8 settings account for?On K3.25 most of the long-code gap goes with W8A16 prefill; on K4 no setting tried closes it (best: W8A16 + amax, −0.0061 of +0.0090).supportedprecision
Does distance from BF16, measured to 14.4K, predict accuracy at long context?Only in part: K3.25-Core-FP8 is farthest and scored lowest at 127K, but compact sits with hqa and anchor on distance and scored below hqa at 127K.indicatedprecision
Can distances from BF16 be compared across studies and CuteAFD releases?Yes on CuteAFD: the same weights give the same rows across v3.0.1, v4.0.0 and v5.0.0 (K3.25-Core-FP8 0.0476 on all three).establishedprecision
Does accuracy on long-context tasks fall with length, and does core precision cause the fall?Yes, in every configuration including the BF16 core (anchor 0.985 at 4K to 0.918 at 127K); much of it is replies that did not finish.supportedlong-tasks
Where does a configuration's precision show up as a cost on long-context tasks?K3.25-Core-FP8 scored 0.051-0.058 below three 4-bit-expert configurations at 127K; nothing was detected at 4K and 16K.supportedlong-tasks
Does the compact attention mode cost accuracy at long context?Against hqa at 127K, yes (+0.037); against the BF16 core, not detected.indicatedlong-tasks
Do long replies (to 130K tokens) finish and pass their checks on both engines?Yes for code and math on both engines (7-8 of 8 at the 130K cap); JSON validity varies; CuteAFD's replies are shorter.supportedlong-tasks
Do the engines accept and use prompts of 256K to 1M tokens?In a one-request pilot, yes: both engines found the needle at 256K-1M tokens.indicatedlong-tasks
Does the core's precision change when the model decides to stop thinking on science questions?Not detected on either engine (11 + 30 questions on the vLLM fork, 41 on CuteAFD); shifts beyond about 0.05 in log p are excluded.establishedthinking
Does the core's precision change how long the model thinks in free generation on science questions?Not known: one question on one engine, inconclusive (0.80 [0.51, 1.14]).openthinking
Why does CuteAFD's BF16-core arm think shorter on code than its native 6-bit-core arm?Mostly CuteAFD's FP8 KDA setting: switching it off lengthened code thinking 1.31x, about the whole gap; on the vLLM fork the two cores think alike (1.00 [0.96, 1.04]).supportedthinking
Does shorter thinking cost correctness on code?Not settled: the 1.26x length gap came with no detected pass-rate difference (+1.6 points [−7.8, +10.9]), but an all-BF16-path arm passed more often in a post hoc look.openthinking
What does CuteAFD v5's default attention mode (hqa) trade against a BF16 core?4,266 MiB less than the BF16 core, with no task cost detected (+0.003 [−0.005, +0.012]).supportedsettings
What does the compact attention mode trade against hqa?2,568 MiB less than hqa, and below hqa at 127K (+0.037).indicatedsettings
What does turning CuteAFD's FP8 settings off cost in speed and memory?W8A16 prefill: about 4% first-token time; BF16 KDA and head: a smaller KV pool (1,931,264 to 1,215,488 tokens on K4-Core-K6), and BF16 KDA did not fit a K4 serving launch on v3.0.1.supportedsettings
Between the two 4-bit checkpoints, K4 and K4-Core-K6, is either closer to the BF16 model?Neither on both sets: level on the teacher panel (which can favour K4), K4-Core-K6 closer on the fidelity set (−0.0049).opencheckpoints
How does the EXLR8 K4 checkpoint compare with the K4-Core-K6 configurations?Not detected to differ on tasks over four passes; farther from BF16 on the fidelity set, level on the teacher panel.supportedcheckpoints
Is CuteAFD faster than the vLLM fork on this machine?On v4.0.0, faster or level in every measured cell on K3.25 and K4 (0 slower); v3.0.1 was slower at 1K with 8 streams and in first token with many streams.establishedspeed
Does K4-Core-K6's 6-bit core cost speed on CuteAFD?Slower than K4 by 5-15% (v4 run); per-cell bands for this contrast were not published.indicatedspeed
How much context fits, and how long does a start take?CuteAFD v4.0.0: larger KV pools (1,931,264 vs 1,377,179 tokens on K4) and ready in 81-91 s against 257-349 s.establishedrunning
Does the same request give the same result twice?CuteAFD: bit-identical across launches, days and releases. vLLM fork: varies beyond its first prefill chunk.establishedrunning
Do the engines reuse a repeated prompt?CuteAFD: yes (0.017 s for a repeated 8K prompt). vLLM fork: only in whole 4,352-token blocks, nothing below 17,408 tokens.supportedrunning
Does multi-turn tool calling differ between the engines or checkpoints?All arms 158-163 of 176; the engines differ on 5-7 of 88 scenarios; CuteAFD repeats exactly.supportedtools
Do leads found after looking at the data hold on new data?Not in these runs: five leads found after looking each shrank or failed on new data or more passes.establishedmethod

What was compared

EnginesCuteAFD (attention/FFN disaggregation runtime; v3.0.1, v4.0.0, v5.0.0) and tpurtell's vLLM fork for this model on two RTX GPUs (v0.9.2; in some runs with a local core loader, labelled where used)
CheckpointsK3.25 (3.25-bit experts, two builds), K4 (brandonmusic's 4-bit experts), K4-Core-K6 and K4-Core-K6-HQA (4-bit experts, 6-bit core), a locally derived BF16-core checkpoint, and kindlingai's EXLR8 K4 as a reference; see checkpoints
Machinetwo RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q Workstation Edition, 300 W; every number on this site is from this one machine
Studies16 runs, dated 2026-10-26 to 2026-11-10 (the last ran until 2026-11-12); see studies

How to use this site

  • Question pages (precision, long tasks, thinking length, settings, checkpoints, speed, running it, tool calling) give the current answer, the judgements behind it, and what was not measured.
  • Studies lists every run, every sub-question it asked and every finding it produced; the v5 and v4 records hold those releases' studies in full.
  • Method says how results are made and how to weigh them; the audit page lists every published statement this rebuild corrected.
  • Machine-readable: findings.json (every finding and judgement with source pointers) and runs.json (every run, its goals and the files each goal owns). Start there rather than with the HTML.
