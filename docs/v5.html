CuteAFD v5.0.0: core precision at long context

CuteAFD v5.0.0 adds ATTENTION_MODE, which runs the HQA checkpoint of GLM 5.3 Flash at two core precisions; a locally derived all-BF16 core is the reference here. On graded tasks from 4K to 127K tokens, neither setting is measurably behind the reference: hqa − anchor 0.000 [−0.012, +0.012] and compact − anchor −0.010 [−0.026, +0.006] in field score, pooled over lengths. Accuracy falls with length in every probe arm, anchor included (0.98 at 4K, 0.89 to 0.91 at 127K), so that loss is not produced by core precision; a third to a half of it is replies that did not finish. One slice leans against the K6 core, running totals at 64K and 127K, and twenty items do not decide it. The eight-turn pass did not test history written by the model; its design is to be revised.

Added after these results were read: a reference checkpoint in another quantisation format, exlr8-k4, on the same items (two single-message passes). No difference from anchor (+0.006 [−0.007, +0.019]) or from hqa (+0.006 [−0.008, +0.019]) is measured; it differs from the arms as a whole checkpoint, so no difference from an arm is attributable to one component (reference checkpoint). Two further single-message passes per arm are in progress under amendment 1; none of their numbers is on this page.

What the results show

  • hqa against the reference: no difference measured, 0.000 [−0.012, +0.012] pooled; request-level disagreement at the level of one arm against its own second pass; nominally lower on long-context running totals (−0.023 [−0.073, +0.023]), interval including 0. Basis: 60 items at four lengths, two passes; strength: moderate (one model family, synthetic tasks).
  • compact against the reference: no difference measured, −0.010 [−0.026, +0.006] pooled. Its largest nominal gap is ledger at 64K and 127K (−0.062 [−0.127, +0.002]); that is open, not shown.
  • hqa against compact: no difference measured, +0.010 [−0.005, +0.025] pooled.
  • The long-context loss is shared. The reference loses about as much as the other arms, so core precision does not produce it; a third to a half of it is replies that did not finish.
  • Split delivery shows no accuracy cost within intervals of about 0.07; whether model-written history does is untested here.
  • Reference checkpoint exlr8-k4, added after the results were read: no difference from anchor or hqa measured (+0.006 [−0.007, +0.019] and +0.006 [−0.008, +0.019] pooled). Against compact its field score is higher post hoc (+0.016 [+0.004, +0.029]), on the partial-credit score only: a lead, not a finding. Each is a whole-checkpoint difference (details).

Tasks. 60 synthetic items (join, ledger, trace; 20 each), each rendered at 4K, 16K, 64K and 127K tokens (length labels 4k, 16k, 64k, 128k in the data); 8 answer fields per item. Each item hides a small set of records among distractors and asks for eight exact values: join looks records up across two tables, ledger keeps running totals over events spread through the context, trace follows a chain of references. The same item is rendered at every length; only the amount of distractor text changes. Per arm: two single-message passes and one pass that delivers the same records over eight turns. Thinking on, temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k -1, one recorded seed per (item, length, pass): distinct within an arm, the same in every arm; reply cap 32,768 tokens; 8 requests at once. Protocol frozen 2026-11-10 19:55:10, seven seconds before the first serving launch and before the first measured request (protocol/FROZEN.json); amendments: none for the graded runs of the three arms; amendment 1 of 2026-11-11 16:46 adds two single-message passes per arm, in progress (protocol/AMENDMENT-2026-11-11.md). Graded runs: 2026-11-10 19:55 to 2026-11-11 08:28.

Terms. The experts are the per-layer expert matrices; the core is the other matrices of the layers (attention among them), and the head is the output projection. K4 and K6 are the checkpoint producer's EXL3 quantisation levels, K6 the finer; BF16 is the original precision. The drafter is the small model that proposes tokens the main model verifies. Prefill is processing the prompt, decode is generating the reply. "Cap" is the reply limit in tokens (32,768 for answers, 4,096 for the delivery turns of the eight-turn pass); "loop" is the engine's repetition abort (finish class repetition).

Score. Fields right / fields, read from the reply's last answer line; a reply that hit the cap or was ended by the loop abort scores 0. Differences are paired by item, with a 95% interval from resampling the 60 items.

Running totals at long context

In the single-message passes, ledger field score at 64K and at 127K orders anchor, hqa, compact. hqa is nominally lower than the reference by 0.023 pooled over the two lengths, [−0.073, +0.023], and every one of its intervals includes 0. compact is nominally 0.07 lower at 64K and 0.06 at 127K with intervals that include 0; pooled over the two lengths the field-score gap is −0.062 [−0.127, +0.002], and only the strict measure excludes 0. Twenty items cannot tell "the K6 core costs nothing on running totals at long context" from "it costs about 0.13 in field score". An extension with more ledger items was written down before the data and is held, not run (note).

Eight turns: what this pass tested

Scope of this pass. Decode ran in every reply of every pass. What the 8-turn pass did not exercise is history written by decode: the model's own turns (thinking included, which stays in context) are a median 9% of the final prompt at 4K, 2.7% at 16K, 0.7% at 64K and 0.3-0.4% at 127K, so at long context more than 99% of the history the final answer was conditioned on was written by prefill. The pass measures split delivery of the same input. It does not test a conversation whose history the model wrote (protocol question 3). The turn design is to be revised.

What the pass does show: delivering the same input as eight messages, each resumed from the prefix cache, shows no accuracy cost. From 16K to 127K every interval includes 0, and a cost of up to about 0.07 is not excluded; at 4K eight turns score slightly higher in anchor and hqa (those two intervals exclude 0).

Checks before the graded runs

Before the graded runs the protocol records five exploratory checks (sections 10 to 13 of the protocol; one capture each, not measurements):

  • Depth profile. Five arms scored the same long texts (to 128K tokens; not the task items) position by position: the three above, fp8c (ATTENTION_MODE=fp8-compact) and core-fp8 (the BF16-core checkpoint with the engine's default FP8 conversions on). No arm was measurably worse than the reference at predicting the text at any depth; the arms differ from each other by about the same small amount, with no ordering by precision.
  • FP8 prefill switch. On the HQA checkpoint, GLM5_FLASH_FP8_PREFILL=off changed the load line and left every recorded distribution bit-identical (11,648 rows), so it was dropped as an arm.
  • Launch to launch. Two launches of anchor and two of hqa gave bit-identical rows (11,648 of 11,648 each): scored rows carry no launch-to-launch noise. Sampled generation still varies between passes.
  • Cache path (hqa, compact, fp8c and hqa with FP8 prefill off; anchor not in this step). A conversation resumed from the prefix cache matched a decode replay bit for bit in 45 of 46 conversations, counted over the four arms together; the exception is a harness artifact (a reply that ended inside the thinking block is rendered with a closing tag the model did not generate). A context prefilled in one piece and the same context built turn by turn are close but never bit-equal, in every arm.
  • Batching (same four arms). Greedy output was one text in all 260 requests per arm, alone or eight at once.

Reference checkpoint: EXLR8 K4

exlr8-k4 is a reference checkpoint added after the results above were read: kindlingai/glm-5.3-flash-exlr8-k2-k3-k4 @ 867580abd28d142bb4c56218bb81a752b7dea988, manifest k4, a native EXLR8 checkpoint: routed and shared experts in a K4 trellis format with E4M3 rounding; attention output, q_b_proj and the dense MLP at K4; attention input projections MXFP8; embedding and head FP8. Same engine release and image, same drafter, the same 60 items at the same four lengths, the same seeds, reply cap, concurrency and sampling settings; the two single-message passes only (2026-11-11 13:24 to 16:21, one launch per pass; no eight-turn pass). 480 requests, 0 errors; prompt tokens equal to anchor's on 480 of 480. The engine loads a native EXLR8 checkpoint from a content-addressed store; for these launches the store (SparkNest v0.8.3, one node) ran on the same host and was mounted there. Upstream's status note says bundled vision and full prefix-continuation exactness are not yet qualified for this checkpoint, which is why it is run single-message only here.

exlr8-k4 is a different checkpoint from the three probe arms, not a setting of the same one. Any difference from a probe arm is a whole-checkpoint difference and is not attributable to one component. It is outside the frozen comparison, and every interval in this section is post hoc. anchor stays the reference of the probe, not a recommendation.

  • exlr8-k4 against anchor and against hqa: no difference measured, +0.006 [−0.007, +0.019] and +0.006 [−0.008, +0.019] pooled. Basis: two passes, 60 items, 480 requests per arm.
  • exlr8-k4 against compact: higher field score, post hoc, +0.016 [+0.004, +0.029] pooled and +0.030 [+0.006, +0.056] at 64K and 127K; at 4K and 16K +0.002 [−0.006, +0.009]. The arm was added after the results were read and this is one of 84 intervals, so it is a lead and not a finding. It is on the partial-credit score only: on every field right the pair does not separate (+0.025 [−0.017, +0.065]). Being a whole-checkpoint difference, it does not say which component carries it.
  • Accuracy falls with length in the reference checkpoint as in the probe arms (0.987 at 4K to 0.934 at 127K; finished replies only at 127K 0.942, anchor 0.942).

Open questions

  • Where does the long-context loss that every probe arm shows, anchor included, come from? The arms share the model, the K4 experts, the engine, the drafter, the sampling settings and the reply cap; a third to a half of the loss is replies that did not finish.
  • Is the K6 core's nominal gap on long-context running totals (ledger at 64K and 127K) real? Twenty items leave both zero and a loss of about 0.13 in field score inside the pooled interval.
  • Is a conversation continued on history the model itself wrote equivalent to the same tokens prefilled? The eight-turn pass did not test this.

Limits

One model family; K4 experts in every arm; contexts to 127K; synthetic tasks; 60 items per length; speeds are per stream under a mixed concurrency-8 load and are not an arm comparison.

Reproducing this

  • Recompute. In the results folder, python3 analysis/posthoc.py (standard library only) rebuilds the scores, differences and intervals on this page from samples.csv and writes POSTHOC.json; python3 tools/verify.py at the repository root checks docs/findings.json against the same table. Run from a clean copy of the published folder on 2026-11-11: same output.
  • Rebuild the inputs. drivers/gen_tasks.py --items 20 --first 100 regenerates the 240 rendered items from their seeds; it needs the tokenizers package and the model's tokenizer.json (set the path at the top of the script; its SHA-256 is checked). items/measured/index.json lists the SHA-256 of every rendered item. Regenerated from the published script on 2026-11-11: 240 of 240 hashes match.
  • Re-measure. chain.sh, drivers/run_tasks.py and configs/ are what ran, with the image digest and checkpoint revisions in RUN.json. The HQA checkpoint is no longer on the hub, so hqa and compact cannot be re-measured without a copy of it; anchor's checkpoint is assembled locally, with every core matrix taken from the original BF16 weights, and was never a release. The items and the scoring do not depend on the engine.
