Precision: how close to the BF16 model?

Both engines stay close to the BF16 model, and the vLLM fork is slightly closer. The difference is largest when reading long code (8-16K-token prompts): CuteAFD is +0.0054 further on K3.25 (coarse KL) and +0.0090 on K4 (a top-20 measure). Below 8K on K3.25, and on long human-written text at 32K-128K on both checkpoints, no difference is resolved. K4 sits about half as far from BF16 as K3.25 on both engines.

"Reading" follows each run's declared rule: K3.25, the 95% interval excludes 0; K4, it also has to exceed the vLLM fork's launch-to-launch difference on the same rows. Band: the largest difference between any two vLLM runs' means on the same rows. CuteAFD repeats are byte-identical, so CuteAFD needs no band.

Notes

  • Why the vLLM fork is averaged over runs. Its prefill is not bit-reproducible beyond the first 2,045-token chunk, so one vLLM run is one draw; CuteAFD's repeats are byte-identical. CuteAFD is paired against the mean of three vLLM runs from two launches (K3.25) or two launches (K4).
  • Length or content. The 8-16K bucket is block B of the set, long code reading; within block A, the only block at every length, the K3.25 KL difference is flat. Which of the two drives the difference is not separated.
  • Projection source inside CuteAFD (K3.25). CuteAFD on Core-FP8 with the official FP8 projections (C1) against CuteAFD on K3.25-v1 with FP8 projections made at load (C0): C1 − C0 −0.0003 [−0.0019, +0.0014], not resolved.
  • Where the panel text came from. P-B's replies were written greedily by an earlier CuteAFD release serving the official FP8 model; any bias from that favours CuteAFD, against the observed difference.
  • Top-20 measures. KL20 reads about 0.8 of full-vocabulary KL on P-A; KL_pb20 keeps 98-99% of coarse KL on P-B generated positions; every K3.25 difference kept its sign and significance under the truncation.
  • Only the prefill path was scored. Tokens generated during decode use another path; its precision was not measured.
