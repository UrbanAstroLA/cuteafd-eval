Precision: how far from the BF16 model, and why

Most of the distance comes from the routed experts. Going from 3.25-bit to 4-bit experts moves a checkpoint several times further toward the BF16 model than switching engines or changing the core's precision does (established). CuteAFD is slightly farther than the vLLM fork on the same checkpoint, most of all on long code (established). On K3.25 most of that long-code gap goes with its FP8 prefill, which a setting can remove for about 4% more first-token time; on K4 no setting tried closes it (supported). Distance at short context predicts long-context accuracy only in part (indicated). The measurements are the prefill path only, to 14.4K tokens of context.

KL from BF16 measures how far a configuration's next-token distribution is from the unquantized model's on the same text. It is measured on the prefill path only (the engine scores given text) and only to 14.4K tokens of context, because no BF16 reference exists beyond that on this machine. Each judgement below cites the findings it rests on and states what it does not show. The strength rules are on the terms page.

Where the distance comes from

Where does most of a configuration's distance from the BF16 model come from: the routed experts, the core, or the engine?

established From the routed experts. On CuteAFD v4.0.0 the step from 3.25-bit to 4-bit experts (K3.25 to K4) changed teacher-panel KL by −0.0195. On the same checkpoint the engine changed it by +0.0017 (K3.25) and +0.0035 (K4). The core moved it less: on the fidelity set hqa was 0.0026 closer than the BF16 core, and on the teacher panel the BF16-core configuration was not the closest (anchor 0.0320, compact 0.0297, intervals overlapping). In the v5 study K3.25-Core-FP8 was farther than every 4-bit-expert configuration on both sets, by +0.0124 to +0.0183 on the fidelity set.

Why this strength: Comparisons planned before their data, on two reference sets (teacher panel, fidelity set) and two engines, point the same way. The expert step is resolved on the teacher panel in the v4 run. The whole-checkpoint step is resolved on both sets in the v5 run (20 of 20 intervals exclude 0). The top-20 ratios agree in direction on both engines and both panels. Engine and core differences are resolved or bounded, and each is under a fifth of the expert step. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000; prefill path; contexts to 14.4K; CuteAFD v3.0.1, v4.0.0 and v5.0.0 and the vLLM fork v0.9.2.

Basis (12 findings) and what it does not show

  • supports CP-k325-bf16-diffs — K3.25-Core-FP8 farther than all four 4-bit-expert configurations, both sets (a whole-checkpoint step: experts and core change together)
  • supports V4-top20-ratio — the same direction on both engines and both panels (descriptive, top-20)
  • context V4-fv-engine-k325 — engine difference on K3.25
  • context V4-fv-engine-k4 — engine difference on K4
  • context CP-bf16-hqa-anchor — core precision: BF16 core against hqa
  • context V4-fv-k6-vs-k4 — K6 core with other 4-bit experts against K4: not detected
  • limits CP-bf16-exlr8 — among 4-bit-expert configurations the two sets order them differently
  • supports K4-vs-k325-level — the same step on the fidelity set and on both engines (top-20, no interval)
  • limits CP-panel-tilt — the teacher panel can favour brandonmusic's K4; the fidelity set's relation to either calibration set is not known, and the v5 rows carry other experts; both point the same way
  • limits V4-top20-k6-vs-k4-pb — between two 4-bit checkpoints with different producers the difference is as large as an engine gap
  • context CP-bf16-teacher — teacher-panel levels of the v5 configurations

Does not show

  • That the same ordering holds on the decode path or beyond 14.4K; nothing was measured against BF16 there.
  • Whether the bit width or the calibration text makes the expert difference: K3.25 and K4 also differ in producer and calibration data.
  • That distance from BF16 predicts task accuracy (see S-distance-tasks).
  • Which of the 4-bit-expert configurations is closest: they sit in one band and the two sets order them differently.
  • That the teacher panel is neutral between checkpoints: it comes from the evidence set of K4's producer.

Engine against engine

On the same weights, is CuteAFD farther from the BF16 model than the vLLM fork?

established Slightly, and most on long code. On the teacher panel (2K contexts) CuteAFD is farther by +0.0017 on K3.25 (v3.0.1 and v4.0.0 give the same rows) and +0.0035 on K4 (v4.0.0). On the fidelity set the difference is resolved in the 8-16K bucket, which is all long-code reading: +0.0054 on K3.25 and +0.0090 on K4 (a top-20 measure, not comparable in level). At 0-2K and 2-8K on K3.25 it is not detected. At 32K-128K there is no BF16 reference; there the engines' NLL on six long documents was not detected to differ (K3.25 −0.0005 [−0.0037, +0.0018]; K4 +0.0076 [−0.0076, +0.0228]).

Why this strength: Frozen rules, two checkpoints, two reference sets and two CuteAFD releases, all in the same direction. On the fidelity set, where the vLLM fork's rows vary between runs, CuteAFD is paired against more than one vLLM run; on the teacher panel the vLLM fork's launches agree to within a millionth. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); prefill path; CuteAFD v3.0.1 and v4.0.0, vLLM fork v0.9.2; K3.25 and K4.

Basis (12 findings) and what it does not show
  • supports V3-pa-engine — teacher panel, K3.25, v3.0.1
  • supports V4-fv-engine-k325 — teacher panel, K3.25, v4.0.0
  • supports V4-fv-engine-k4 — teacher panel, K4, v4.0.0
  • supports V3-pb-longcode — fidelity set 8-16K, K3.25
  • supports K4-pb-longcode — fidelity set 8-16K, K4 (top-20)
  • limits V3-pb-short — 0-2K: not detected
  • limits V3-pb-mid — 2-8K: not detected
  • limits V3-u1-blockB — the 8-16K bucket is one kind of text, so length and content are not separated
  • context V3-q2-nll — 32K-128K, engine against engine, K3.25
  • context K4-q2-nll — 32K-128K, engine against engine, K4
  • context V3-i1-baseline — the vLLM reference varies between runs; the gap holds against each
  • context V3-f3-pa-launch — teacher panel: two vLLM launches agree

Does not show

  • Which engine is closer to BF16 beyond 14.4K tokens: no reference exists there.
  • That the gap grows with context length: within the one kind of text present at every length, growth was not detected (V3-u1-blockA-trend).
  • That the gap costs task accuracy: no task study paired the engines on the same checkpoint at long context.

CuteAFD's FP8 settings

What each setting costs in speed and memory, on every engine version, is on the settings page.

How much of CuteAFD's long-code gap do its FP8 settings account for?

supported On K3.25 (v3.0.1), most of it goes with the FP8 prefill: W8A16 prefill moved the 8-16K KL by −0.0032, leaving +0.0022 against the vLLM fork; with KDA, head and prefill FP8 all off it moved by −0.0038 and the remainder, +0.0016 [−0.0005, +0.0033], is not detected. The BF16 head alone moved it by −0.0003. On K4 the same W8A16 switch moved −0.0019 of a +0.0090 gap; W8A16 with the amax scale rule moved −0.0061 and all BF16 paths −0.0050, and each still left a resolved gap (+0.0030 and +0.0041); BF16 KDA projections alone moved −0.0035.

Why this strength: Follow-ups declared after the engine result and before their own requests, on one item set (the fidelity set's 8-16K bucket, 18 windows) on each of two checkpoints. The checkpoints disagree on how much the prefill accounts for, so the result is per checkpoint. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); prefill path; CuteAFD v3.0.1; K3.25 and K4.

Basis (9 findings) and what it does not show
  • supports V3-f1-w8a16 — K3.25, W8A16 prefill
  • supports V3-f1-all — K3.25, all three settings off
  • context V3-f1-head — K3.25, BF16 head: resolved, very small
  • context V3-f1-kda — K3.25, BF16 KDA: not detected
  • limits K4-f1-w8a16 — K4, W8A16 prefill
  • limits K4-c-w16amax — K4, W8A16 + amax
  • limits K4-b-all16 — K4, all BF16 paths
  • context K4-b-kda16-head16 — K4, KDA and head singly
  • limits V3-m6-prefill-only — only the prefill path was scored

Does not show

  • What these settings do on the decode path: only prefill was scored.
  • What they cost; that is on the settings page (S-set-costs).
  • Why K4's gap is mostly elsewhere: KDA output sharding, index-key format, attention placement and prefill geometry also differ between the engines (V3-u2-arith).

Does distance predict long-context accuracy?

Does distance from BF16, measured to 14.4K, predict accuracy at long context?

indicated Only in part. K3.25-Core-FP8, the farthest of the five v5 configurations from BF16, also scored lowest at 127K. But compact sits in the same distance band as hqa and anchor, yet among the 4-bit-expert configurations it alone scored lower at 127K, and resolved only against hqa (+0.037 [+0.004, +0.073]). Up to 16K no configuration's score differed from the others'.

Why this strength: One study. Compact's difference rests on one four-pass interval among 102. The 127K tasks have no BF16 reference, so distance and task score are measured at different lengths. Scope: CuteAFD v5.0.0; five configurations; synthetic graded tasks 4K-127K; distance to 14.4K.

Basis (6 findings) and what it does not show
  • context CP-bf16-distance — How far is each of the four configurations from the BF16 model on the fidelity set?
  • supports CP-k325-bf16-diffs — Is k325-fp8 farther from BF16 than each of the four other configurations?
  • supports CP-k325-tasks — At 127K, does k325-fp8 score differently from the four other configurations (four passes)?
  • supports CP-fourpass-compact-127k — Over four passes at 127K, does hqa score differently from compact?
  • limits CP-fourpass-compact-anchor — compact is not resolved from anchor
  • context CP-k325-tasks-short — level at 4K-16K

Does not show

  • That short-context distance is irrelevant to long context.
  • Why compact or K3.25-Core-FP8 lose at 127K; the losses are mostly replies that did not finish (v5 study, endings).

Comparing across studies

Can distances from BF16 be compared across studies and CuteAFD releases?

established Yes, for the same weights on CuteAFD. v4.0.0 rows are bit-identical to v3.0.1. K3.25-Core-FP8's teacher-panel KL is 0.0476 on v3.0.1, v4.0.0 and v5.0.0. K4-Core-K6's 0.0297 on v4.0.0 reappears for compact on v5.0.0, which loads the same K6 tensors. A second v5.0.0 launch repeated every row exactly.

Why this strength: Exact agreement across three releases and repeated launches. Scope: CuteAFD v3.0.1, v4.0.0 and v5.0.0; the teacher panel and the fidelity set; prefill path.

Basis (5 findings) and what it does not show
  • supports V4-v3-identity — Does CuteAFD v4.0.0 give the same teacher-panel rows as v3.0.1?
  • supports CP-k325-earlier-release — Does the same checkpoint measure the same distance on CuteAFD v3.0.1 and v5.0.0?
  • supports V4-fv-k6-vs-k4 — K4-Core-K6 on v4.0.0
  • supports CP-bf16-teacher — compact on v5.0.0
  • supports CP-bf16-repeat — Does a second launch reproduce the BF16-distance measurement exactly?

Does not show

  • That the vLLM fork's rows repeat: its multi-chunk prefill varies from run to run (v3.0.1 audit I1), so its rows are paired against the mean of several runs.
  • That other CuteAFD settings or checkpoints would repeat across releases; only these were compared.

Not measured

  • Distance on the decode path, the path generated tokens take. Every row above scores given text.
  • Distance beyond 14.4K tokens of context. At 32K-128K only engine-against-engine NLL exists.
  • Other model families, tokenizers, GPUs or power limits. KL and NLL are per tokenizer, so these numbers are not comparable with any other model's.
