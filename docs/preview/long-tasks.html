Long tasks: long prompts and long replies

Accuracy on graded long-context tasks falls with length in every configuration, the BF16 core included (supported); much of the fall is replies that did not finish. Precision showed as a task cost at 127K, for some configurations: K3.25-Core-FP8 against three 4-bit-expert configurations (supported), and compact against hqa but not against the BF16 core (indicated); nothing was detected at 4K and 16K. Long replies to 130K tokens finish and pass their code and math checks on both engines (supported, 8 replies per cell). Prompts of 256K-1M tokens were accepted and used in a one-request pilot (indicated).

Accuracy against length

Does accuracy on long-context tasks fall with length, and does core precision cause the fall?

supported It falls in every configuration, the BF16 core included: over four passes anchor went from 0.985 at 4K to 0.918 at 127K, hqa 0.984 to 0.924, compact 0.981 to 0.887 and exlr8-k4 0.983 to 0.920. Part of the fall is replies that did not finish: on finished replies at 127K, two passes, the three probe arms score 0.928 to 0.942, and finished join replies at 127K score 0.989 to 0.997. Accuracy by evidence position stayed within about 0.05 at every length.

Why this strength: A frozen protocol with four passes on one synthetic item set (60 items) and one engine; the fall has the same direction in all five configurations. One item set, so supported, not established. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v5.0.0; generation with thinking on; synthetic join, ledger and trace items at 4K-127K.

Where precision costs accuracy

Where does a configuration's precision show up as a cost on long-context tasks?

supported K3.25-Core-FP8 scored −0.051 to −0.058 below anchor, hqa and exlr8-k4 at 127K, and was not detected to differ from any of them at 4K and 16K; pooled over all four lengths it was −0.012 to −0.016 below the same three. More of its join replies at 127K did not finish.

Why this strength: A frozen rule (amendment 2, written before its requests) on one item set; the unfinished-reply shares were computed after the results were read. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v5.0.0; graded synthetic tasks at 4K-127K.

Does the compact attention mode cost accuracy at long context?

indicated Against hqa, yes at 127K: hqa − compact +0.037 [+0.004, +0.073], and +0.013 [+0.002, +0.024] pooled over all lengths. Against the BF16 core it is not detected: compact − anchor −0.031 [−0.070, +0.008] at 127K. More of compact's join replies at 127K did not finish than hqa's.

Why this strength: Resolved against hqa only, in the four passes of amendment 1 (passes 3-4 added after the two-pass results were read); in neither pass set alone is the pooled difference resolved. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v5.0.0; K4-Core-K6-HQA; graded synthetic tasks.

Long replies

Do long replies (to 130K tokens) finish and pass their checks on both engines?

supported Code replies at the 130K cap passed all tests on 7 of 8 per engine on K3.25 and 8 of 8 on K4, and level-5 math problems were correct 8 of 8 on both. JSON validity varied between runs and engines (K3.25: 6 and 4 of 8; K4: 5 and 3 of 8). Under the audit's loop rule no reply looped; replies the detector flagged copy code out of the reasoning. CuteAFD's replies were shorter (code median 38,190.5 against 51,297.5 tokens on K3.25). K4-Core-K6 finished fewer replies within 32K than K4 (11 and 13 against 17 and 18 of 32).

Why this strength: Frozen protocols on three runs (K3.25 and K4 on v3.0.1, K4 and K4-Core-K6 on v4.0.0, two seeds) give the same picture, but as counts of 8 replies per cell with no intervals. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v3.0.1 and v4.0.0, vLLM fork v0.9.2; thinking on; one prompt per content type and cap, 8 replies per cell.

  • context V3-l-length — reply length
  • context V4-blockL-k6-r1 — K4-Core-K6
  • context V4-blockL-r2-k6 — K4-Core-K6, second seed

Very long prompts

Do the engines accept and use prompts of 256K to 1M tokens?

indicated In an exploratory pilot on K3.25, one request per prompt, both engines finished all 12 requests and found the single code at 256K, 512K and 1M tokens; first token at 1M took 269.7-270.5 s (vLLM fork) and 254.2-255.4 s (CuteAFD).

Why this strength: Exploratory, one request per cell, no protocol. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v3.0.1 and vLLM fork v0.9.2; K3.25; prompts of 256K-1M tokens.

Basis (2 findings) and what it does not show
  • supports LP-needle — retrieval
  • context LP-ttft — first-token time

Does not show

  • Accuracy on harder long-context tasks at these lengths.
  • Anything about K4 or later engine versions.

Not measured
