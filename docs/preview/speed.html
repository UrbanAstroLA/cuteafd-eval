Speed

CuteAFD v4.0.0 is faster than the vLLM fork or level with it in every cell measured on K3.25 and K4 (established for this machine). v3.0.1 was slower at short prompts with many streams and in first-token time at 1K and with many long prompts. K4-Core-K6 runs slower than K4 on CuteAFD (indicated).

Engine against engine

Is CuteAFD faster than the vLLM fork on this machine?

established On v4.0.0, faster or level in every cell measured on K3.25 and K4 (aggregate faster in 19 and 22 of 22 cells, slower in none), and K4-Core-K6 on CuteAFD was faster or level against the vLLM fork running K4 (20 of 22 faster); time to first token at 128K 19.8 s against 34.9 s on K3.25. On v3.0.1 CuteAFD was faster in single-stream decode (1.02 to 1.41) and in aggregate at 32K-128K, but slower in aggregate at 1K input with 8 streams (0.87 to 0.93), in first token at 1K (0.40-0.41 s against 0.25-0.26 s) and in first token at 32K-128K with 4-16 streams (1.18-1.47).

Why this strength: Frozen speed grids with drift-repeat noise bands on two CuteAFD releases and two checkpoints with both engines on the same weights; the v3.0.1 exceptions are named. K4-Core-K6 has no vLLM counterpart, so its cells compare different checkpoints. Scope: GLM-5.3-Flash on two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q (300 W); CuteAFD v3.0.1 and v4.0.0, vLLM fork v0.9.2; prompts 1K-128K, 1-16 streams, thinking on.

The 6-bit core

Does K4-Core-K6's 6-bit core cost speed on CuteAFD?

indicated The v4 run reports K4-Core-K6 5-15% slower than K4 across the speed cells; its own vLLM-relative decode at one stream is 0.99-1.36x against K4's 1.07-1.42x. In the core-swap run (exploratory) the native 6-bit core decoded 120.9 tok/s on code against 129.8 for the BF16 core and 129.4 for K4.
