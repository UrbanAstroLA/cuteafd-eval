Three checkpoints: K3.25, K4 and K4-Core-K6

Two producers, two calibration mixes. tpurtell's K3.25 and K4-Core-K6 share one calibration set weighted to code, maths and function calling; the calibration windows published for brandonmusic's K4 add legal text. Going from K3.25 to either 4-bit checkpoint removes most of the confident mistakes on the teacher panel. Between the two 4-bit checkpoints neither is closer to BF16 on both panels: K4 on legal and reasoning text, K4-Core-K6 on tpurtell's fidelity set.

What they were calibrated on

Calibration is the text a quantizer looks at while deciding how to round. A checkpoint can keep more precision on text like its calibration set. Both producers give about a third to code; the visible difference is legal text (in the windows published for K4) against maths and function calling (tpurtell).

A difference where BF16 itself was unsure is usually a different but reasonable word. A difference where BF16 was confident is a likely mistake; in context these are mostly specific recall (statute numbers, names, dates). The same checkpoint on the two engines differs in 40 (K3.25) and 43 (K4) top tokens per 1,000, almost all where BF16 was unsure.

K4 and K4-Core-K6

Same 4-bit expert budget, different producer, calibration and core. On the panel drawn from brandonmusic's corpus they tie on average, with K4 closer on legal and reasoning-termination text. On tpurtell's set K4-Core-K6 is closer. K4-Core-K6 also leaves 16% more room for context and runs 5-15% slower (a later rerun puts the decode slowdown in its 6-bit core). On code, its 6-bit core and the original BF16 core think about as long on the vLLM fork (1.00x on 12 code prompts) and with CuteAFD's native 6-bit kernels; CuteAFD's BF16 core converted to FP8 at load thinks about a fifth shorter, with tests passing as often; the routed experts add nothing measurable (Thinking length).

Measures

  • KL: how far the checkpoint's next-token probabilities sit from BF16's, in nats; 0 = identical.
  • Top token differs: the checkpoint's most likely next token is not BF16's.
  • P-A: 25 sealed windows of 2,048 tokens with full-vocabulary BF16 teacher logits (general, legal, code/agentic, reasoning-termination). P-B: tpurtell's fidelity set, BF16 top-k rows, generated positions.
  • Resolved: the 95% interval (window bootstrap) excludes 0.
